# Survival Realism – make it feel real

## 1. The Zombie Rulebook (mandatory, shown or implied in every video)
Fill before designing visuals and **never break it**:
- **Cause/origin** (virus, fungus, unknown – keep vague unless script needs).
- **Senses:** sound? sight? smell? (default: **sound + movement first**, sight at ~20–30 m in light, poor in darkness).
- **Speed:** walker / runner mix? stamina?
- **Infection:** bite/scratch? time to turn (e.g., 6–12 h)? Can people hide it? (keep non-graphic).
- **Weakness:** head trauma? fire? water? daylight? (pick few).
- **Behaviour:** herds follow noise? sleep when no stimulus? weather effects?
- **Scale:** how many zombies in this area vs. survivors?
State rules via a **rule card** early ("RULE #1: Noise = Death"), then **obey**. Consistency = realism = trust.

## 2. Resource Ledger (track every beat)
Maintain numbers and show them when they change:
`water (litres) · food (days) · ammo/weapon durability · batteries/fuel · medicine · people (healthy/injured) · hours until dark · distance to goal`
- Loss must be **earned/caused** (spilled, used, stolen, broken).
- Never refill for free. Replenishing needs a **supply run with risk**.
- Show ledger as an inventory HUD or a hand-written list; update on screen when numbers change.

## 3. Consequence rules
- **Noise travels** (visualise as expanding ring/wave; zombies' heads turn).
- **Light attracts** at night (torch cone/candle visible through windows).
- **Wounds don't vanish:** limp, bandage, fever, slowed pace across later beats.
- **Fatigue & hunger** cause mistakes (dropped item, misread map, argument).
- **One mistake chain:** dropped can → echo → 3 turn → 12 follow → barricade strain. Show the *domino*.
- **Time passes:** day counter, shadows lengthen, candle shortens, beard/dirt grows.
- **Morale:** show faces, silences, small rituals (shared meal, music box, diary).

## 4. Believable behaviour
- People **hesitate, whisper, check exits first**, move along walls, close doors softly, avoid mirrors/glass, avoid stairwells' echo, sleep in shifts.
- Group decisions are messy; give **two valid options with trade-offs** (stay vs go; save one vs risk all).
- Avoid: infinite ammo, silent gun use, heroic one-liners mid-horde, perfect aim, characters ignoring wounds, zombies ignoring rules for drama, convenient miracles.

## 5. Survival Facts (safe & accurate – use on screen as "Survival Rule" cards)
Hedge with "general guidance". Use only if relevant.
- **Priorities (Rule of 3s, rough):** ~3 minutes without air, ~3 hours in extreme weather without shelter, ~3 days without water, ~3 weeks without food.
- **Water first:** store clean water; **boil ~1 minute at a rolling boil** (longer at high altitude) or use proper purification tablets/filters; avoid flood/stagnant water.
- **Shelter:** high floors/rooftops with limited access, strong doors, **two exits**, avoid ground-level glass.
- **Noise & light discipline:** cover windows, use low lights, quiet footwear, avoid metal clatter.
- **Move in daylight, rest in dark;** plan route & fallback; keep a **go-bag** (water, food, light, first aid, ID copies, radio, charger).
- **First-aid basics (general):** stop bleeding with firm pressure, clean wounds, keep dry; seek professional care when possible. No dosing or medical claims.
- **Fire/CO safety:** never burn fuel/charcoal in closed rooms; ventilation is vital.
- **Sanitation:** hygiene prevents real disease; handwashing, safe toilet spot away from water & shelter.
- **Signalling/comm:** hand-crank radio, mirror flashes, written notes; agree rally points.
Do **not** present instructions for making weapons, explosives or harmful devices.

## 6. Realistic pacing of a survival story
Outbreak (chaos) → Escape → First shelter → Supply crisis → Contact (other humans) → Betrayal/loss → Relocation → Last stand/hope. Use any segment; keep chronology clear via day counter.

## 7. Plausibility checks
- Can a normal person do this with this gear? If not, make it hard and costly.
- Is the setting's layout logical (doors, stairs, vents, water tanks, power)?
- Are distances & times plausible (walking ≈ 4–5 km/h; sprint short; carrying weight slows)?
- Do injuries, weather, light level match earlier beats?
