# Sound Design – half of the "realism"

Design sound per beat (it shapes visual rhythm). Visual cut often lands **on a sound**.

## Layers
1. **Room tone / ambience** – wind, rain, distant traffic-less hum, insects, fridge hum, power buzz. Silence is a *sound choice*: use "dead air" rarely for max effect.
2. **Foley** – footsteps (surface-specific), cloth rustle, breathing, zips, keys, can clink, door creak, chain rattle, bandage tear.
3. **Threat sounds** – groans (distinct per type), scratching, shuffling drag, distant bang, screamer shriek (short, sharp), glass crack.
4. **Tension drones** – low sub-bass swell, slow pulse, heartbeat that speeds up with proximity, high thin tone (tinnitus-like) at peaks.
5. **Diegetic tech** – radio static & clicks, battery beep, phone vibration, CCTV whirr, generator sputter.
6. **Music** – sparse; ambient pads, dissonant strings, piano single notes, no constant beat until chase; **drop music for the scare**, bring warm acoustic guitar/flute for human moments.

## Rules
- **Sound before sight:** let viewers hear it 1–2 s before they see it.
- **Distance & occlusion:** muffled (low-pass) behind doors/walls; louder & brighter in open.
- **Silence then hit** (but avoid over-using the "quiet-loud" trick).
- **Heartbeat tied to distance** between survivor and threat.
- **Noise ring visual** pairs with a distinct "tick" sound.
- Voice: whisper-level dialogue in danger; nothing heroic and loud.
- Keep levels safe: avoid ear-piercing spikes; normalise peaks (≈ −3 dBFS) & use limiter.

## Per-type motifs
Walker – low wet groan + drag · Runner – breath rasp + quick footfalls · Crawler – scrape on floor · Screamer – rising shriek · Heavy – deep thud steps · Stalker – almost silence with faint breathing.
